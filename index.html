The problem

Clinical AI companies drown their own agents in context.

Patient data arrives from EHRs, lab reports, imaging, clinical PDFs, wearables, doctor notes and agent conversations, fragmented across sources, duplicated across visits, and accumulating over years. Most teams hand all of it to the agent and hope it finds what matters.

The architecture

Four layers, one continuously updating patient state.

Named for the brain's own relay station: the structure that filters and routes signal to the right destination. Thalamus does the same for clinical data.

L1

Raw clinical data

Every document a patient generates, kept in full. Nothing is discarded at this layer.

L2

Atomic clinical data points

Every document broken into discrete, tagged clinical facts, each one linked back to its source document, timestamp and provenance.

L3

Computed patient state

Active vs. resolved conditions, current medications, disease control, longitudinal trends, all inferred from the atomic layer and kept current as new data arrives.

L4

Intelligence layer

Compiles the right context for each agent, matches patient state against clinical guidelines, and surfaces care gaps before an agent starts reasoning.
